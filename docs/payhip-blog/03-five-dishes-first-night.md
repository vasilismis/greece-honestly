# Five dishes to order on your first night in Greece

Your first night in Greece. You have found a taverna, the table is outside, and the menu is long. Where do you start?

Do it the Greek way: order several dishes for the table and share them. Here are five that make a perfect first meal, from the first bite to the last.

## 1. Tzatziki

Start with the classic. Yoghurt, cucumber and garlic, cool and sharp. It goes with almost everything that comes later, so order it first and keep it on the table. Bread will usually arrive too; it is normally charged, so say so if you do not want it.

## 2. Horiatiki

The real "Greek salad": tomato, cucumber, onion and olives, with a thick slab of feta on top. No lettuce. One bowl is plenty for two or three people.

## 3. Fava

Not the broad beans you might expect. Greek fava is a smooth purée of yellow split peas, usually finished with onion and olive oil. Gentle, filling, and a good choice if anyone at the table is vegetarian.

## 4. Saganaki

Cheese, fried in a small pan until it is golden outside and soft inside. It arrives hot, so eat it straight away.

## 5. Gemista or souvlaki

For something more substantial, choose one of these:

- **Gemista**: vegetables, usually tomatoes and peppers, stuffed with rice and herbs and baked. A favourite home-style dish.
- **Souvlaki**: pieces of meat grilled on a skewer. Simple and hard to get wrong.

## Then, if there is room

Loukoumades: small fried dough balls with honey. Sometimes something sweet simply appears at the end. If the waiter says *kerasméno*, it is on the house, a gift from the taverna.

## Three things to know before the bill

- **Ask for the bill.** Say *To logariasmó, parakaló* (the bill, please).
- **You should get a receipt**, and card payment should be possible.
- **Tipping is optional.** About 5–10% in cash if you were happy.

And if you order fish, remember that it is often priced by the kilo. Ask for the weight and the price before it is cooked.

*Kalí órexi!* Enjoy your meal.
