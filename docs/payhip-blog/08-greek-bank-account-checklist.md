# The Greek bank account: what to carry through the door

Opening a bank account in Greece is rarely difficult. Being sent home for a missing paper is the common problem. Here is the folder to bring, and the reason behind each item.

## The folder

1. **Passport or ID card.** The original.
2. **Your AFM.** Your Greek tax number. If you do not have one yet, that is your first job, not the bank.
3. **Proof of address in Greece.** Usually your registered lease, or a utility bill in your name. Make sure the address is current, because the bank may post your card and letters there.
4. **Your residence permit,** if you are not an EU citizen.

Banks differ, and some may ask for more, such as proof of income or your AMKA. Phone or ask in the branch for their exact list before your appointment.

## The order that works

Most of these papers depend on each other. The smoothest path is:

- **AFM first,** through myAADE.
- **Then a lease,** which your landlord declares to AADE and you accept online.
- **Then the bank,** with everything in one folder.

## If the bank says no

EU rules give consumers who are legally resident in the EU the right to a **payment account with basic features**. Banks must not refuse because of nationality or place of residence, though they must refuse where opening the account would break anti-money-laundering rules. If you are turned down, ask for the reason in writing, and ask specifically about a basic payment account. Greece made these rules part of its own law (Law 4465/2017), but how each bank applies them still varies, so be persistent and polite.

## While you wait

- Keep your **home bank account** open until the Greek one works.
- Keep your **home phone number** active, as security codes may still go there.
- Check what your home bank charges for paying and withdrawing in euros.

## Questions worth asking at the desk

- What does the account cost each month?
- How long until I get my card and online banking?
- Can I receive my pension or salary from abroad into it?
- Do you need originals, copies or translations?

Five minutes of questions can save you a second trip.
