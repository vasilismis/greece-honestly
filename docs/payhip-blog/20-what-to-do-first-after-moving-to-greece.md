# What should I do first after moving to Greece?

Get your **AFM**. Then everything else falls into place in a sensible order. Here is the sequence that saves you second trips to the same office.

## 1. AFM and authentication key

The AFM is your Greek tax number, and almost everything else asks for it: the lease, the bank, many official steps. Apply through **myAADE**, then confirm your identity by video call or at a tax office. The AFM itself is free. Ask for the **authentication key** at the same time, so you can use AADE's online services.

## 2. A Greek phone number

Banks and services send codes by text. Take your passport to a phone shop: SIM cards in Greece are registered to your identity.

## 3. A home with a registered lease

Your landlord declares the lease to AADE and you accept it online. That registered lease becomes your **proof of address**. Remember that a main-residence lease has a three-year minimum that binds you too.

## 4. A bank account

Take your passport or ID, your AFM, proof of address and, if you are not from the EU, your residence permit. Greek law gives legally resident consumers a right to a basic payment account.

## 5. AMKA

Your social security number, issued in person at a KEP or e-EFKA. EU citizens bring a passport or ID; holders of the Financially Independent Person permit generally cannot get one and keep private health insurance instead.

## 6. Your residence

- **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens** register in person with the police service for foreigners after three months.
- **Non-EU citizens** apply online for their residence permit before the national visa expires.

## Three habits that help

- Keep one folder, paper or digital, with your passport, AFM, lease and every certificate.
- Check the public holiday calendar before booking appointments.
- Save 112, the European emergency number.
