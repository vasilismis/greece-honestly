# Ferry day in Greece: a calm checklist

Greek ferries are part of the holiday: the deck, the wind, an island growing on the horizon. A little preparation keeps ferry day relaxed instead of rushed.

## The night before

- **Check your booking.** Can you board with the e-ticket on your phone, or do you need to collect a paper ticket at the port? Almost all companies use e-tickets now, but not every case.
- **Check the port.** Big cities can have more than one ferry port. Make sure you know which one your boat leaves from.
- **Check for messages.** Wind can delay or cancel sailings, especially in the Cyclades in July and August. Look at your email and phone.

## In your bag

- Your **passport or ID card**, the original. Tickets are in your name.
- Proof of any **discount** you booked, such as a student card.
- Water, a snack and a jumper. Decks get windy, and air-conditioned lounges can be cold.

## At the port

- Aim to arrive **about an hour before departure**. Some companies want foot passengers on board half an hour before sailing, and vehicles earlier.
- Ticket booths are typically open at least an hour before departure, if you need to collect a ticket.
- Keep your ticket handy. It is checked as you board and sometimes during the voyage.

## Planning smarter

- **Never put a ferry and an international flight on the same day** if you can avoid it. Keep your last night near your departure airport.
- **Stay on one ferry line.** Islands in the same group connect easily.
- **Around 15 August, book early.** It is peak season.
- **Out of season, expect fewer sailings.** Some connections stop after mid-October.

## Where to book

Use the ferry company's own site, a booking site or app that compares several companies, or the port and local agents. Whatever you use, compare departure and arrival times, the type of boat and the port, not just the price.

Ferry times change every year. Check the current schedule for your dates, then enjoy the crossing.
