# Which Ionian island can I enjoy without a car?

The Ionian islands are green, the winds are softer than in the Cyclades, and many towns show their Venetian past. But some of them really need a car, and some do not. Here is the quick answer.

## No car needed: Paxos

**Paxos** is small and quiet, with no airport: you arrive by ferry from Corfu or Igoumenitsa. You can manage without a car. The best-known day out is by boat to tiny **Antipaxos**, with traditional boats leaving Gaios in the morning and returning in the afternoon. One warning: on windy days the crossing can be very uncomfortable, so do not hire a self-drive boat then.

## Car optional: Corfu

**Corfu** has direct flights and suits families and first visits. If you base yourself in **Corfu Town**, its Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage site that you explore on foot. For beaches around the island, a car helps, because Corfu is big.

## Car recommended: Ithaca

**Ithaca** has no airport; the main crossing is Sami (Kefalonia) to Pisaetos, about 20 to 30 minutes. Buses are limited, so a car or scooter helps. Several beaches, such as Gidaki and Platia Ammos, are best reached by boat anyway.

## Car needed: Lefkada and Kefalonia

**Lefkada** is the island you drive to, over a floating bridge from the mainland, and its best beaches are far apart. **Kefalonia** is the biggest of the six and mountainous, with long drives between areas: the road down to Myrtos alone has many tight bends.

## Car useful: Zakynthos

**Zakynthos** has direct flights and a year-round ferry from Kyllini. A car is useful to explore beyond your resort.

## If you do hire a car

Check that your contract allows ferries before you book a crossing, and allow more time than the map suggests on mountain roads.
