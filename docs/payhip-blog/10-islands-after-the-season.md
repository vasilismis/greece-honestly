# After the season: picking a Greek island in October or November

Every autumn someone books a beautiful small island for November and arrives to find the tavernas closed and the ferry running twice a week. It does not have to be you. One rule avoids it:

**Big islands keep living. Small islands go to sleep.**

## October and November, in short

- **October** is quieter. On small islands, some places start to close.
- **From November to March**, small islands go to sleep, while big islands keep their year-round towns, shops and transport.

## Four names to remember

- **Crete.** The safest bet. Its northern towns stay active all year, and flights from Athens run all winter.
- **Rhodes.** Rhodes Town stays lively year-round; the resort areas go quiet, so stay in or near town.
- **Corfu.** Hotels, restaurants and businesses work all year, but the Ionian is rainy in winter. Pack a proper jacket.
- **Syros.** One of the islands that keeps living outside summer. Check ferry days carefully.

## The one to avoid

Islands that live almost entirely from summer tourism close down sharply. Out of season, Mykonos's shutdown is described as almost total.

## Getting there

Ferries run all year, but with **fewer sailings**, and some connections stop after mid-October. For an autumn trip, the calm approach is to **fly** to one big island, then treat any ferry hop as a bonus. Ferry times change every year, so always check the current schedule for your dates.

## A three-point autumn checklist

1. Choose a big island.
2. Confirm your hotel and the places you want to eat are open on your dates.
3. Pack for rain and cooler evenings.

Do that, and autumn on the islands is calm, unhurried and very Greek. You get the light, the walks and the tavernas that stay open, without the summer queues.
