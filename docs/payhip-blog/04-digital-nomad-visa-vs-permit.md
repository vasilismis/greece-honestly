# Digital nomad visa vs the 2-year permit: what changes

People often talk about "the Greek digital nomad visa" as if it were one thing. In practice there are two stages: a **national visa** you get before you travel, and a **residence permit** if you want to stay longer. They do different jobs, and mixing them up is how people end up with gaps in their paperwork.

## Stage 1: the national visa (type D)

- **Where:** at a Greek consulate, before you travel. Since Law 5275/2026, Greek immigration lawyers report that arriving as a tourist and applying later is no longer accepted.
- **How long:** valid for up to **12 months**.
- **Fee:** the national visa fee set in the law is **€75**. The consulate will tell you about any other charges.
- **What you show:** at least **€3,500 a month in net income** (+20% for a spouse, +15% per child), with employers or clients **outside Greece**, and **full health insurance covering Greece**. Travel insurance is not enough.

Think of the visa as your entry ticket: it lets you move to Greece and start living and working remotely there.

## Stage 2: the residence permit

- **How long:** **2 years**, and **renewable**.
- **Where:** in Greece, through the Ministry of Migration's procedures. Your consulate and the ministry set out the steps and fees.
- **When:** keep an eye on your visa's expiry date, and do not leave it to the last minute.

Think of the permit as your longer-term base.

## So what actually changes between the two?

| | National visa | Residence permit |
|---|---|---|
| Where you get it | Greek consulate, before travelling | In Greece |
| How long it lasts | Up to 12 months | 2 years, renewable |
| Main purpose | Getting you to Greece legally | Staying longer |

The income, the outside-Greece work rule and the insurance requirement apply throughout. Your plans for the next few years are what decide whether you need the second stage.

## The tax question does not wait for the permit

Whichever stage you are at, spending more than 183 days in Greece in any 12-month period, or making it your home, normally makes you a Greek tax resident, taxed on your worldwide income. And the 50% tax break you may have heard about usually does not fit digital nomads: it requires working for a Greek employer or a Greek permanent establishment.

Get tax advice early, not when your first Greek tax return is due.
