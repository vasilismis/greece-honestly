# Do I need an AMKA, and can I get one?

If you are moving to Greece, you will hear about two numbers: the **AFM** (your tax number) and the **AMKA** (your social security number). The AFM is for almost everyone. The AMKA depends on who you are and what permit you hold.

## What the AMKA is for

The AMKA is the number the Greek social security and public health systems use to identify you. You need it to use the public health system, including registering an **S1** form, and a non-EU citizen generally needs both an AFM and an AMKA before starting work in Greece.

## Where you get it

In person, at a **KEP** (citizens' service centre) or a branch of **e-EFKA**, the social security body. A representative with a certified authorisation can also apply for you.

## Who can get one

- **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens:** bring your passport or ID card. Take your AFM and proof of address too; guides commonly mention them.
- **Non-EU citizens:** the official AMKA site lists a valid passport and a **residence permit that gives access to the labour market**.
- **Financially Independent Person permit holders:** that permit does not allow work, and guides report that its holders generally **cannot** get an AMKA. They keep private health insurance instead.
- **Asylum applicants:** receive a temporary number (PAAYPA), which becomes an AMKA if the application is accepted.

## UK and EU pensioners with an S1

The order is: request the S1 from your home country (UK pensioners: NHS Overseas Healthcare Services, +44 191 218 1999), get your AMKA at a KEP, then register the S1. GOV.UK names EOPYY for that last step, while EOPYY's own cross-border page names e-EFKA, so ask at the KEP which office to use. A new S1 comes every year and must be registered each time.

## AMKA does not equal free healthcare

Your access depends on your insurance: Greek social insurance through work, or an S1 from your home country. The number on its own is the key, not the cover.

## Before you go

Ask the KEP which documents to bring, and whether they need an official Greek translation.
