# How many Greek islands can I visit in ten days?

Two, maybe three. That is the honest answer, and it surprises people who have seen five-island itineraries online.

## Why fewer is better

- **Arrival and departure days eat time.** Two nights on an island often means one real day.
- **Ferries are not buses.** Times change every year, connections between small islands do not run daily, and wind can delay or cancel sailings, especially in the Cyclades in July and August.
- **Crossing between island groups means detours.** Islands in the same group connect easily; jumping from one group to another rarely does.

So the rule of thumb is **at least three nights per island**, **two or three islands**, **one ferry line**, and the **last night near your departure airport**.

## Three ten-day shapes that work

**Classic Cyclades:** Paros (3 nights), Naxos (4), Santorini (3), then fly back to Athens.

**Quiet Cyclades:** Sifnos (4), Milos (3), Folegandros (3). Check that the small-island connections run on your dates.

**Ionian by car:** fly into Corfu (3), take the ferry to the mainland and drive over the floating bridge to Lefkada (4), ferry on to Kefalonia (3) and fly home. Check the Lefkada–Kefalonia ferry for your date.

## Before you book anything

1. Check this year's ferry schedules for your exact dates.
2. Book the accommodation only once the connections are confirmed.
3. Never put a ferry and an international flight on the same day.

## What about out of season?

From October the picture changes. Ferries still run all year, but with fewer sailings, and some connections stop after mid-October. Small islands go to sleep from November to March, while big islands such as Crete, Rhodes, Corfu and Syros keep living. In autumn, one big island plus a single ferry hop is usually wiser than a chain of small ones.

Ten days on two or three islands is not "seeing less". It is seeing them properly.
