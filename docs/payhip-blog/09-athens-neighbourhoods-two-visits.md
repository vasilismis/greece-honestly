# Choosing an Athens neighbourhood: the two-visit rule

Athens rewards people who look before they sign. The same street can feel calm at 8 in the morning and very different at 11 at night. That is why we suggest a simple rule: **visit every shortlisted area twice, once on a weekday morning and once in the evening.**

Here is a quick sketch of five areas newcomers often consider, to help you build that shortlist.

**Koukaki.** Central and walkable, close to the Acropolis, with cafés and small shops. Popular with newcomers, and it has become one of the pricier central areas. Ask how many flats in the building are let to tourists.

**Pangrati.** Close to the centre but calmer, leafy and residential, with good local tavernas. Often recommended for families and long stays.

**Kypseli.** North of the centre, lived-in and everyday, often described as one of the more affordable central options. Quality varies a lot by street, so view carefully.

**Glyfada.** On the coast, upscale, popular with families including those choosing international schools. Higher rents and a longer trip into the centre.

**Kifisia.** A leafy northern suburb, family-oriented and among the more expensive areas for larger flats. Check the commute before you commit.

## Three questions before you fall in love with a flat

1. **How will you get around?** Central areas are easier without a car; suburbs often are not.
2. **How does the flat cope with summer?** Ask about cooling and air conditioning.
3. **What is the building like?** Shared charges, the lift, noise, and who your neighbours are likely to be.

## And before you sign

The paperwork is the same everywhere: you need an **AFM**, your landlord **declares the lease to AADE** and you accept it online, and for a main residence the minimum term is **three years**. Read the contract carefully, agree the deposit in writing, and photograph the flat on moving day.

Pick the area for the life you will actually live, not for the photo you saw online.
