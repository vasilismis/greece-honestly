# Can I leave a Greek lease before three years?

Not as easily as many newcomers assume. This is the part of Greek renting that surprises people most, so it is worth understanding before you sign.

## The three-year rule

For a home you live in as your main residence, Greek law sets a **minimum lease term of three years**. It applies even if the contract you sign says one year, and, crucially, it **binds both sides**: the landlord and you.

## Shortening it

The three years can only be shortened by a **new notarial deed** signed **at least six months after** the lease starts. A clause in the original contract is not enough.

## What happens if you just leave

If you move out before the three years are up, the landlord can claim the **rent until the end of the three years**, or at least until the flat is let to someone new. That can be a lot of money.

## How to protect yourself

- **Be honest about your plans.** If you might leave within three years, say so before you sign, and agree in writing what happens if you do.
- **Ask a lawyer** to read the contract if anything about the term or leaving early is unclear.
- **Keep the paperwork clean.** The landlord must declare the lease to AADE, the tax authority, by the end of the month after it is agreed, and you accept it online in myAADE with your AFM. A registered lease also becomes your proof of address.
- **Agree the deposit in writing.** Deposits are commonly one or two months' rent. Do not try to "use up" the deposit by not paying the last months' rent; agree how it will be settled instead.

## On moving day

Take dated photos of every room, list the furniture and appliances with both signatures, and note the meter readings. If you ever do leave early, a clean record makes every conversation easier.

## The short version

Three years means three years, for you as well as the landlord. Plan for it, or negotiate it in writing.
