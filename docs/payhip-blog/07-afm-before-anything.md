# Why the AFM comes before everything else in Greece

Ask anyone who has moved to Greece what they wish they had known, and many will say the same thing: **get your AFM first.**

The AFM is your Greek tax identification number, issued by AADE, the tax authority. It sounds like something only accountants care about. In practice, it is the key that opens the next doors.

## The doors it opens

- **A flat.** Long-term leases are declared to AADE by the landlord, and the declaration includes the tenant's AFM. You then accept the lease online.
- **A bank account.** Banks ask for it.
- **Your proof of address.** Your registered lease becomes the proof of address for the bank and for registering your residence.

Without an AFM, that chain never starts.

## Good news: you can start online

You apply through **myAADE**, AADE's online portal. Then you confirm your identity, either by a **video call** with an AADE officer or **in person** at a tax office. People who do not yet live in Greece can apply too, and a lawyer, accountant or authorised person can apply on your behalf.

## The bit people forget

Ask for your **authentication key** at the same time. The AFM is the number; the key is what lets you log in to AADE's online services. You will need it to accept your lease, among other things.

## What the AFM is not

It is not a residence permit, and having one does not on its own make you a Greek tax resident. Tax residence depends on things like where your permanent home and centre of life are, and whether you spend more than 183 days in Greece in any 12-month period.

## A simple order for your first weeks

1. AFM and authentication key
2. A Greek phone number
3. A flat, with the lease registered
4. A bank account
5. AMKA, then residence registration or your permit application

Do it in that order and you will rarely be sent away to come back with "one more paper".
