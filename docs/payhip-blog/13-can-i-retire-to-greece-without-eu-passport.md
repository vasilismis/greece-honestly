# Can I retire to Greece without an EU passport?

Yes, and thousands of people do. The route most non-EU retirees use is the **Financially Independent Person permit**: a residence permit for people who live on income from outside Greece, such as pensions, rents and dividends.

## The income test

You need to show at least **€3,500 a month**. For a couple that rises by 20% to **€4,200**; each child adds 15%, so a couple with one child needs **€4,725**, and with two children **€5,250**.

## Two stages, in this order

1. **A type D national visa**, applied for in person at the Greek consulate where you legally live.
2. **The residence permit itself**, applied for online on the Ministry of Migration platform before the visa runs out, followed by biometrics. The fees are €1,000 for the main applicant, €150 for each adult family member and €16 per card.

The permit lasts **three years** and can be renewed for three more. Apply in the two months before it expires. If you are late, you can still apply up to three months after expiry, but it costs €100 for every month of delay.

## The two conditions people miss

- **No work in Greece.** Not for you, not for your family members: no job and no self-employment. Foreign pensions, rents and dividends are fine.
- **Full health insurance covering Greece.** Travel insurance does not count. Because the permit gives no access to the labour market, holders generally cannot get an AMKA (the social security number), so private insurance is your healthcare.

## And the tax question

Once Greece is your home, or you spend more than 183 days there in any 12-month period, you are normally a Greek tax resident. Many retirees then look at the **7% flat tax** on foreign income, which has its own conditions and an application deadline of 31 October. Talk to an accountant before you move, not after.

## The short answer

No EU passport? Show €3,500 a month (more for a family), get the type D visa first, apply for the permit in Greece, keep private health insurance, and do not work in Greece.
