Autumn is a lovely time for the Greek islands. The crowds have gone and the pace slows down. But the season ends, and on many islands it ends quickly. Choose the wrong island and you may find the tavernas shuttered and the ferry running twice a week.

The rule of thumb is simple: **big islands keep living, small islands go to sleep.**

:::facts
calendar | October | Quieter; some places close on small islands
leaf | November | Small islands go to sleep
pin | Best bets | Crete, Rhodes, Corfu, Syros

:::

## October: the shoulder month

October is quieter than summer. On the **small islands**, some hotels, tavernas and beach businesses start to close during the month. On the big islands, life carries on much as normal.

October can still be a great month for a beach holiday on a big island, especially early in the month. Check that the specific hotel and restaurants you want are open on your dates.

## November to March: small islands go to sleep

From November, **small islands go to sleep.** Many businesses close until spring, and the people who stay are mostly locals. That can be wonderful if you want silence and walks, but it is not a typical holiday.

**Big islands keep living.** They have year-round towns, shops, hospitals and transport, so a visit works in any month.

## Four islands that work out of season

| Island | Why it works in autumn |
|---|---|
| **Crete** | The biggest island; its northern towns, such as Chania, Rethymno and Heraklion, stay active all year. Flights from Athens run all winter. |
| **Rhodes** | Rhodes Town stays lively all year; flights from Athens run all winter. Resort areas go quiet. |
| **Corfu** | Has hotels, restaurants and businesses that work all year round, but it is wetter in winter. |
| **Syros** | One of the islands that keeps living outside summer. Check ferry days carefully. |

::figure cyclades-harbour | A small island harbour. Out of season, ferry days matter more than ever.

### Crete

If you only remember one name, make it Crete. It is big enough to have its own year-round life, with towns that stay busy, and it is reached by plane as well as ferry. It is also among the warmer places in Greece in winter.

### Rhodes

Rhodes Town, with its old walled centre, stays lively all year. Resort areas on the coast go quiet out of season, so base yourself in or near the town.

### Corfu

Corfu keeps hotels, restaurants and tourist businesses open all year, and it has direct flights. The catch: the Ionian islands are rainy in winter, so pack for it.

### Syros

Syros is one of the islands that keeps living outside summer. It is a good choice if you want a Cycladic island out of season, but check the ferry schedule for your dates before you book.

:::note Avoid in late autumn
Islands that live almost entirely from summer tourism shut down sharply. Mykonos is the best-known example: out of season its shutdown is almost total. Lovely in silence, but do not expect the summer island.
:::

## Ferries and flights out of season

- **Ferries still run all year**, but with **fewer sailings**. Some routes and connections stop after the middle of October.
- **Ferry times change every year**: always check the current schedule for your exact dates.
- **Flights from Athens** to Crete and Rhodes run through the winter.

The safest autumn plan is one big island, reached by plane, with ferry hops as a bonus rather than the backbone of the trip. For more on booking, see [Greek ferries: how to book](/articles/greek-ferries-how-to-book/).

## What to pack for October and November

- a light jacket or jumper for the evenings
- a rain jacket, especially for the Ionian

## Quick checklist

- Choose a **big island**: Crete, Rhodes, Corfu or Syros.
- Check that your **hotel and restaurants** are open on your dates.
- Check **ferry days** for your dates; there are fewer sailings.
- Prefer a **flight** for the main leg of the journey.
- Pack for **rain** and cooler evenings.
