A meal in a Greek taverna is one of the best parts of any trip to Greece. It is also slightly different from eating out at home. People order differently, some things are priced differently, and the bill has a few surprises if you do not know them.

Here is how it works, plus ten dishes worth knowing.

## How Greeks order: share everything

The biggest difference is this: **Greeks order several dishes and share them**. Instead of one starter and one main each, the table orders a few things and everyone helps themselves.

A simple approach for two people:

- a dip or two
- a salad
- one or two hot dishes
- one main dish to share

You can always order more later. Over-ordering is the classic tourist mistake.

## Ten classics, explained

| Dish | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Tzatziki** | A cool dip of yoghurt, cucumber and garlic. Good with bread and grilled meat. |
| **Horiatiki** | The "Greek salad": tomato, cucumber, onion, olives and a slab of feta. Usually no lettuce. |
| **Fava** | A smooth purée of yellow split peas, often topped with onion and olive oil. Not the same as broad beans. |
| **Gemista** | Vegetables, usually tomatoes and peppers, stuffed with rice and herbs and baked. |
| **Moussaka** | Layers of aubergine and minced meat, topped with a thick béchamel sauce, then baked. |
| **Souvlaki** | Pieces of meat grilled on a skewer. |
| **Gyros** | Meat cooked on a vertical spit and sliced thin, often wrapped in pitta. |
| **Kleftiko** | Lamb cooked slowly until it falls off the bone. |
| **Saganaki** | Cheese fried in a small pan until golden. |
| **Loukoumades** | Little fried dough balls with honey. A dessert, sometimes with nuts or cinnamon. |

Recipes vary from region to region and from kitchen to kitchen, which is part of the fun. If you are not sure what something is, ask.

## Things that work differently

### Bread is usually charged

When you sit down, bread often arrives without you asking. It is usually charged, so if you do not want it, say so politely when it comes.

### Tap water is common

Many tavernas bring a jug or bottle of tap water to the table. If you prefer bottled water, ask for it.

### Fish is priced by the kilo

Fresh fish is often priced **by the kilo**, not per portion. That means the price on the menu is not the price of your plate.

To avoid a surprise:

1. Choose your fish.
2. **Ask for the weight and the price before it is cooked.**
3. Agree, then let them cook it.

This is completely normal in Greece, and nobody will mind you asking.

### Frozen ingredients must be marked

Frozen ingredients must be marked on the menu. Look for a small note or symbol next to the dish. It is especially useful to know when you order seafood.

## Vegetarians and allergies

Two useful words:

- **Chortofágos** means vegetarian. "Íme chortofágos" means "I am vegetarian".
- **Alergikós** (for a man) or **alergikí** (for a woman) means allergic.

Many classic dishes are naturally vegetarian, such as horiatiki, fava, gemista and many vegetable dishes. Even so, always ask, because stock, cheese or meat can turn up in unexpected places. If you have a serious allergy, consider carrying a written note in Greek.

## How the bill works

### Asking for it

In Greece, the waiter usually will not bring the bill until you ask. This is hospitality, not slow service: nobody wants to rush you. When you are ready, catch their eye and say **"To logariasmó, parakaló"** (the bill, please).

### Receipt and card payment

- **You should get a receipt.**
- **Card payment should be possible.**

If you are not offered a receipt, it is fine to ask for one.

### On the house

Sometimes a dessert, fruit or a small glass of something arrives that you did not order. If the waiter says **"kerasméno"**, it means it is on the house, a gift from the taverna. Enjoy it and say thank you: **"Efcharistó!"**

### Tipping

Tipping is **optional**. If you were happy, about **5–10% in cash** is a kind gesture. Leaving cash on the table is common, even if you paid the bill by card.

## A quick checklist

- Order several dishes and share.
- Expect a charge for bread.
- For fish, ask the weight and price before cooking.
- Look for frozen ingredients marked on the menu.
- Ask for the bill when you are ready.
- Expect a receipt and the option to pay by card.
- Tip 5–10% in cash if you want to.

## Useful words at the table

- **Kalí órexi:** enjoy your meal
- **Yiá mas:** cheers
- **Éna neró, parakaló:** a water, please
- **To logariasmó, parakaló:** the bill, please
- **Efcharistó:** thank you
