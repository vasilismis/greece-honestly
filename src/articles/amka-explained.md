After your tax number, the **AMKA** is the second number that follows you around in Greece. You need it for the public health system and for many dealings with the state. Getting it is usually quick, but what you need to bring depends on where you are from and on your residence status.

This article explains what the AMKA is, who can get it, and how. It is general information, not legal advice.

## What the AMKA is

**AMKA** stands for *Arithmós Mitróou Koinonikís Asfálisis*, the social security registration number. It is the number the Greek social security and public health systems use to identify you.

You will need it to:

- use the **public health system**, including registering an S1 form if you have one
- work in Greece: a non-EU citizen generally needs both an AFM and an AMKA before starting work
- deal with many public services

## Where to get it

The AMKA is issued **in person** at:

- any **KEP**, the citizens' service centres found in towns across Greece, or
- a branch of **e-EFKA**, the national social security body.

You can also apply through a representative with a certified authorisation.

Gather documents | Passport or ID, and your residence document if you are not from the EU.

:::

## What to bring

### EU, EEA and Swiss citizens

Bring your **passport or national ID card**. Guides also commonly mention your **AFM** and **proof of address** in Greece, so take them with you.

### Non-EU citizens

The official AMKA site lists:

- a valid **passport** or travel document, and
- a valid **residence permit that gives access to the labour market**.

:::note What if my permit does not allow work?
Some permits, such as the Financially Independent Person permit, do not allow work in Greece. The official list above mentions a permit with access to the labour market, so ask the KEP or e-EFKA what applies to you **before** you go. Take your permit, passport, AFM and proof of address with you.
:::

### Asylum seekers and people with international protection

Asylum applicants receive a temporary number called **PAAYPA** with their applicant card. If the application is accepted, it is converted into an AMKA; if it is rejected, it is deactivated. The Ministry of Migration and Asylum's guidance explains the steps and time limits.

## If you have an S1 form

UK and EU state pensioners often have healthcare cover in Greece through an **S1 form** from their home country. The order is:

1. Ask your home country for the S1. For UK pensioners, that is NHS Overseas Healthcare Services (+44 191 218 1999).
2. Get your **AMKA** at a KEP.
3. Register your S1 with **EOPYY**, the national health provider.

A new S1 comes every year, and each one must be registered.

## Translations

Documents in other languages may need an official Greek translation. One exception that often helps: some public documents issued by EU countries are covered by an EU regulation that removes the need for translation. Ask the KEP before you go which documents need translating.

## Common questions

**Is the AMKA the same as the AFM?**
No. The AFM is your tax number, issued by AADE. The AMKA is your social security number. You will usually need both.

**Can I apply online?**
Applications are made in person, at a KEP or e-EFKA, or through a representative.

**Does having an AMKA give me free healthcare?**
Not on its own. Your access depends on your insurance: for example, Greek social insurance through work, or an S1 from your home country registered with EOPYY.
