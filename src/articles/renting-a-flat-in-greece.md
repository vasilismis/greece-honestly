Renting a flat is usually the first big step after moving to Greece. The system is not complicated, but it works differently from the UK and many other countries. The lease has to be registered with the tax authority, there is a minimum term that surprises many newcomers, and much of what protects you depends on what is written in the contract.

This article explains how long-term renting works and what to check before you sign. It is general information, not legal advice. For anything unusual, ask a Greek lawyer before you sign.

## Before you start: get your tax number

You will need a Greek tax number, the **AFM**, to rent a flat. Your landlord has to declare the lease to AADE, the Greek tax authority, and the declaration includes the tenant's AFM. You then accept the lease online in myAADE, which you can only do with an AFM and access to AADE's online services.

If you do not have one yet, get it first. Our [first 90 days in Greece](/articles/first-90-days-in-greece/) article explains how.

## The minimum term: three years

For a home you live in as your main residence, Greek law sets a **minimum lease term of three years**. This applies even if the contract states a shorter period. A longer term can be agreed.

What this means in practice:

- **For you:** you have security. The landlord cannot normally end the lease simply because a shorter date in the contract has passed.
- **Leaving early:** sources disagree on exactly how the three-year rule works when the tenant wants to leave early. Make sure the contract says clearly how you can end the lease and with how much notice, and ask a lawyer if anything is unclear.

## The deposit

Landlords usually ask for a deposit. It is commonly **one or two months' rent**, but the amount is agreed between you and the landlord. Put these points in the contract:

- the amount of the deposit
- what it can be used for (for example, unpaid rent or damage beyond normal wear and tear)
- when and how it will be returned at the end of the lease

:::note Good to know
Do not try to "use up" the deposit by not paying the last months' rent. Agree in writing how it will be settled instead.
:::

## The contract

A lease in Greece does not have to be long, but it should be clear. Check that it includes:

- the full names and AFMs of the landlord and tenant
- the address and description of the property
- the start date and the term
- the monthly rent, when it is paid and how (a bank transfer leaves a record)
- any rent increases during the lease and how they are calculated
- the deposit terms (see above)
- who pays utilities and shared building charges
- what furniture and appliances are included, and their condition
- how either side can end the lease, and the notice required

If the contract is only in Greek, get a trusted translation before you sign. Do not sign something you do not understand.

## Registering the lease with AADE

Long-term leases must be declared to AADE. This is how it works:

1. **The landlord files the declaration** in myAADE. The deadline is the end of the month after the lease is agreed.
2. **The declaration includes** the AFMs of landlord and tenant, the lease dates, the monthly rent, the property's details, the electricity supply number and the property's **energy performance certificate**.
3. **You accept it online** in myAADE, in the property rental statements service.

Registration matters to you as a tenant. A registered lease is your official proof of address, which you will need for many other steps, from opening a bank account to registering your residence. Some tax benefits for tenants also depend on the lease being declared.

Ask the landlord when they will file the declaration, and check it in myAADE once they have. Make sure the rent and dates match what you agreed.

## What to check before you sign

**The flat**

- Does everything work: hot water, heating, air conditioning, windows, shutters, appliances?
- Is there damp or mould? Look in wardrobes and corners, especially in older buildings.
- How is the flat heated in winter, and what does that cost to run? Ask the landlord or previous tenant.
- How are the utilities set up? Find out whose name the electricity and water accounts are in.

**The building**

- What are the shared building charges, and who pays which part?
- Is there a lift, parking or storage, and are they included?

**The paperwork**

- Is the person you are dealing with the owner, or authorised to act for them?
- Does the property have an energy performance certificate? It is needed to register the lease.
- Will the landlord register the lease with AADE?

**On moving day**

- Take dated photos and videos of every room and of any existing damage.
- Make a written list of furniture and appliances and their condition, and have both sides sign it.
- Note the electricity and water meter readings.

## A simple order of events

1. Get your AFM and access to myAADE.
2. View flats; check the building, the heating and the paperwork.
3. Agree the rent, deposit and term, and get it all in a written contract.
4. Sign, pay the deposit by bank transfer, and record the flat's condition.
5. The landlord registers the lease with AADE; you accept it in myAADE.
6. Use the registered lease as proof of address for your next steps.
