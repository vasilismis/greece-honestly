Lefkada is the Ionian island you can drive to. There is no ferry to book and no timetable to worry about: you cross a short floating bridge from the mainland and you are on the island. That makes it easy to reach, but it is not the whole story. Lefkada has a wild, cliff-lined west coast, a calm and green east coast, a bay famous among windsurfers and mountain villages where life goes on much as it always has.

This guide covers what you need to plan a trip: how to get there, which beaches suit you (and how to reach them today), where to stay and when to go.

## Getting there

### By road: the floating bridge

Lefkada is joined to the mainland by a causeway and a floating swing bridge across the Lefkada canal. You drive straight across, so the island works well as part of a road trip. The bridge also swings open at set times to let boats through the canal. Road traffic is not affected by its closures to boats, but the opening schedule for boats changes, so sailors should check the current notice.

### By air: Aktion (Preveza) airport

The nearest airport is Aktion, also called Preveza airport, on the mainland. It is about 20 km by road from Lefkada town, and the drive crosses the bridge. Check which airlines fly there from your home airport, and when.

### By bus from Athens

KTEL Lefkadas runs buses from Athens' Kifissos bus terminal to Lefkada town. The journey takes about five and a quarter hours. Check current departure times on the KTEL Lefkadas website before you travel; the number of buses varies through the year.

## The west coast: big views, big waves

The west coast faces the open Ionian Sea. This is where the famous photos come from: white cliffs, very bright turquoise water and sunsets over the sea. It is also more exposed. On windy days the waves can be big, and some beaches are hard to reach.

| Beach | What it is like | How you get there today |
|---|---|---|
| **Porto Katsiki** | A long white beach under high cliffs in the south-west | Drive to the clifftop car park, then walk down a long flight of steps. Not suitable for pushchairs or wheelchairs. Arrive early in summer, as parking fills up. |
| **Kathisma** | One of the easier west-coast beaches to reach | By car, about 3 km from Agios Nikitas |
| **Milos** | A beautiful beach next to Agios Nikitas with no road | Walk the path from Agios Nikitas (about 20–30 minutes, rough in places) or take a water taxi from the village beach in season |
| **Egremni** | A long, wild beach below the cliffs | **By boat is the safe choice.** See below. |

### Egremni after the earthquake

In November 2015 a strong earthquake hit Lefkada and destroyed the road and the steps down to Egremni. A new staircase was built and reopened in 2021. In 2026, local reports said winter storms had damaged the staircase again and that land access had been closed because the ground was unstable. Whatever you read online, treat land access as uncertain. Boat trips from Vasiliki and Nydri visit this coast, and they are the safest way to see Egremni. Ask locally before you go.

## The east coast: calm water and boat trips

The east coast looks across to the mainland and a scattering of small islands. The water is usually calmer, the beaches are smaller and the coast is greener. This is the side for families with young children, for sailing and for boat trips.

### Nydri and the islands

Nydri is the main base for boat trips. Day trips usually head to **Meganisi**, a small island with sea caves (including the large Papanikolis cave), and around **Skorpios**, the private island once owned by the Onassis family. Most trips show you Skorpios from the water and stop to swim nearby; check with the operator what is included. A small local ferry also runs from Nydri to Meganisi, daily in high season, if you would rather explore it on your own.

## Vasiliki: wind for windsurfers

Vasiliki, in the south, is famous among windsurfers. In summer a thermal wind builds in the bay in the afternoon as the land heats up. Mornings are usually calmer, which suits beginners and lessons, while the afternoon wind suits more experienced riders. The windsurfing season runs from about late May to October. Even if you never touch a board, Vasiliki is a pleasant harbour village, and it is a starting point for boats along the south and west coasts.

## The mountain villages

Drive up from either coast and the island changes. The centre of Lefkada is mountainous, with olive groves, small fields and stone villages.

- **Karya** is the best known. It is famous for a type of embroidery, the *karsaniko* stitch, made by the women of the village.
- **Eglouvi**, higher up, is known for its small lentils, grown on tiny plots by a few farmers. They are the island's most famous food product.

Walking between the villages is a lovely way to spend a cooler day. The roads are narrow and winding, so drive slowly.

## Food and local products

Look out for these on menus and in local shops:

- **Eglouvi lentils**, often served simply as a soup or salad
- **Lefkada salami**, a local cured sausage with a long history on the island
- **Sweets** such as nougat and *pasteli* (sesame and honey bars), and the sweet liqueur **rosoli**

For how to order once you sit down, see [what to order in a Greek taverna](/articles/what-to-order-in-a-greek-taverna/).

## Lefkada town

Lefkada town sits right by the bridge and lagoon. Walk the old streets and look up: many houses have a stone ground floor and a light timber upper floor, often covered in painted metal sheets. This local way of building was developed to cope with the island's earthquakes, and it gives the town its colourful look.

**Two cultural notes:**

- **The August festivals.** Lefkada's arts and literature events have been held since 1955, and the International Folklore Festival since 1962. The folklore festival takes place in August and brings dance and music groups from many countries to the town. Check the exact dates each year.
- **Lafcadio Hearn.** The writer Lafcadio Hearn, famous for his books about Japan, was born on Lefkada in 1850. His middle name, Lafcadio, comes from the island's name.

## When to go

- **May–June:** the sweet spot. Warm, green and not yet crowded.
- **July and August:** hot and busy, especially around 15 August, a Greek national holiday. Book early.
- **September:** warm sea and calmer days. One of the best months.
- **October:** quieter, and the season starts to wind down.

**About the wind:** in summer, the west coast can get big waves on windy days, which is when the calm east coast comes into its own. In Vasiliki the wind usually arrives in the afternoon.

## Where to stay

| Your trip | Try |
|---|---|
| Sunsets and the west-coast beaches | Agios Nikitas |
| Boat trips and plenty of choice | Nydri |
| Windsurfing, or ferries to Kefalonia and Ithaca | Vasiliki |
| Shops, restaurants and the bus to Athens | Lefkada town |
| Peace and quiet | A mountain village, or Meganisi |

You will want a car to explore. The island is big and hilly, and the best beaches are far apart.

## Day trips

- **Kefalonia:** a ferry runs from Vasiliki to Fiskardo in season. It does not run every day, so check the timetable for your dates.
- **Ithaca:** ferries link Lefkada (Vasiliki or Nydri) with Frikes on Ithaca in season. Again, check current schedules.
- **Parga:** a pretty mainland town about 85–90 km away by car. You can go and come back in a day, but the roads are winding, so start early.
