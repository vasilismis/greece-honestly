The first weeks in Greece are full of paperwork, and most of it depends on something else. The bank wants your tax number and proof of address. The proof of address is your lease. The lease needs your tax number. Do things in the wrong order and you will find yourself going back to the same office twice.

This article puts the main steps in a sensible order. It is general information, not legal advice. Your exact path depends on your nationality and your residence route, so always check the official pages linked at the end.

:::steps
AFM first | Your tax number unlocks the lease, the bank and most other steps.
Home, phone and bank | A registered lease is your proof of address.
AMKA and residence | Social security number, then register or apply for your permit.
:::

## Before you arrive

- **Non-EU citizens:** you normally need a type D national visa from the Greek consulate before you travel. See our articles on [retiring to Greece](/articles/retiring-to-greece-2026/) and the [digital nomad visa](/articles/greece-digital-nomad-visa-2026/).
- **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens:** you can travel with your passport or ID card. You will register your residence later (step 6).
- **Everyone:** bring your passport and any documents your residence route asks for. Ask the consulate whether they need official translations or an apostille.

## Step 1: your tax number (AFM)

The **AFM** is your Greek tax identification number. Almost everything else needs it: the lease, the bank account, utility contracts and AMKA.

- You apply through **myAADE**, the tax authority's online portal.
- You then confirm your identity, either by **video call** with an AADE officer or **in person** at a tax office.
- You can also apply through a representative, such as a lawyer or accountant.

At the same time, ask for your **authentication key** (the personal code for AADE's online services). You will need it to accept your lease online and for most dealings with the tax authority.

Having an AFM does not, by itself, make you a Greek tax resident. Tax residence depends on where your home and centre of life are, and on whether you spend more than 183 days a year in Greece.

## Step 2: a Greek phone number

A Greek mobile number makes everything easier: banks, landlords, deliveries and many online services send codes by text.

- Take your passport to a mobile phone shop. Greek SIM cards, including prepaid ones, are registered to your identity, so the shop will record your ID.
- Keep your home number active for a while too, in case your bank back home sends you security codes.

## Step 3: somewhere to live

For your first weeks a short let is fine, but for many next steps you need a **registered lease** as proof of address.

- Your landlord declares the lease to AADE, and you accept it online in myAADE using your AFM.
- The registered lease is your proof of address for the bank and for registering your residence.

Read our guide to [renting a flat in Greece](/articles/renting-a-flat-in-greece/) before you sign anything.

## Step 4: a bank account

Greek banks usually ask for:

- your passport or ID card
- your **AFM**
- **proof of address**, such as your registered lease or a utility bill in your name
- for non-EU citizens, your **residence permit**

Requirements vary from bank to bank, so ask for their list before you go. Make sure the address on your documents is current, as the bank may post things to you.

## Step 5: AMKA, your social security number

The **AMKA** is your social security number. You need it for the public health system and for many dealings with the state.

- You get it in person at a **KEP** (citizens' service centre) or a branch of **e-EFKA**, the social security body.
- **EU citizens** bring their passport or ID card.
- **Non-EU citizens**: the official AMKA site lists a valid passport and a residence permit that gives access to the labour market. If your permit does not allow work (the Financially Independent Person permit, for example), ask the KEP or e-EFKA what applies to you before you go.

**UK and EU pensioners with an S1 form:** once you have your AMKA, register your S1 with **EOPYY**, the national health provider. A new S1 comes every year, and you must register each one.

## Step 6: registering your residence

- **EU, EEA and Swiss citizens:** if you stay longer than **three months**, you must register in person with the police. In Attica and Thessaloniki that is the Aliens Directorate; elsewhere, the local police. There is no online procedure. Bring your passport or ID, proof of address, photos, and proof of your situation: work, or, if you are not working, sufficient resources and full health insurance. The certificate does not expire.
- **Non-EU citizens:** apply for your residence permit online on the Ministry of Migration platform **before your national visa expires**, then give your biometrics. Our [retiring to Greece](/articles/retiring-to-greece-2026/) article explains the Financially Independent Person permit.

## A 90-day plan

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Before you go | Visa (non-EU), documents, translations |
| Week 1–2 | AFM and authentication key; Greek phone number |
| Week 2–6 | Find a flat; sign and register the lease |
| Week 4–8 | Bank account; AMKA; S1 registration if you have one |
| By day 90 | EU citizens: register your residence. Non-EU: permit application filed before your visa expires |

Some steps will take longer than you expect, especially in summer and around holidays. Start early, keep copies of everything, and keep a folder (paper or digital) with your passport, AFM, lease and every certificate you receive.

## Emergency numbers

Save these in your new Greek phone on day one: **112** (European emergency number), **166** (ambulance), **100** (police) and **199** (fire).
