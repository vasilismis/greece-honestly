Greece has a lot of islands, and from home they can all look the same: blue sea, white houses, a taverna by the harbour. In reality they are very different. Some are bare and windy, some are green and gentle, some are lively all year and some go to sleep in winter.

The easiest way to choose is to start with the island **groups**. Islands in the same group tend to share a landscape and a style, and they are usually linked by ferry. Pick your group first, then your island.

## The seven island groups

### Cyclades

The classic postcard: white villages, bare hills and blue sea. The Cyclades are windy in July and August, which keeps you cool on land but can make ferry crossings bumpy.

**Islands include:** Santorini, Mykonos, Naxos, Paros, Milos and many small ones.

### Ionian

On the west side of Greece. The Ionian islands are green, with softer winds and towns shaped by the Venetians. A good choice if you prefer trees to bare rock.

**Islands include:** Corfu, Kefalonia, Zakynthos, Lefkada, Ithaca, Paxos.

### Crete

The biggest Greek island, almost a small country in its own right. It has two international airports, Heraklion and Chania, which makes it easy to reach. You could spend weeks here and not see it all.

### Dodecanese

Close to Turkey, with knights' castles and a lot of history.

**Islands include:** Rhodes, Kos, Symi, Patmos.

### North Aegean

Big, green and less touristy. These islands suit travellers who want real local life and do not mind fewer tourist services.

**Islands include:** Lesvos, Chios, Samos, Ikaria.

### Saronic

Close to Athens: 1–2 hours by boat from Piraeus. Ideal if you only have a few days.

**Islands include:** Aegina, Hydra, Poros, Spetses.

### Sporades

Pine forests running down to the sea.

**Islands include:** Skiathos, Skopelos, Alonissos.

A few patterns stand out. Naxos and Paros keep coming up because they work for first-timers and families alike. Crete appears almost everywhere because it is big enough to be several holidays at once. And if you hate ferries, the fly-in list gives you plenty of choice.

## When to go

The month you travel changes an island more than you might expect.

- **April:** green and fresh, but the sea is still cold.
- **May–June:** the sweet spot. Warm, not yet crowded.
- **July:** hot and busy. The meltemi winds blow in the Cyclades.
- **August:** peak season. Around 15 August, a Greek national holiday, is the busiest time of all.
- **September:** the sea is warm and things calm down.
- **October:** quieter. On small islands, some places start to close.
- **November–March:** the small islands go to sleep. The big ones keep living.

If you can only travel in winter, choose a big island with year-round life, such as Crete, Rhodes, Syros or Corfu. A tiny island in January can be beautiful, but you may find most of it shut.

## Three questions to narrow it down

**1. How will you get there?** If you want to fly straight in, start with the fly-in list above. If you are happy to take a ferry from Athens, the Cyclades and Saronic islands open up.

**2. Bare and dramatic, or green and gentle?** Bare and dramatic points to the Cyclades. Green and gentle points to the Ionian, the North Aegean or the Sporades.

**3. Busy or quiet?** The well-known names are well known for a reason, but they are also the busiest. For a calmer trip, look at the smaller islands in the same group.

## One island or several?

If you want to visit more than one island, keep it simple. At least three nights per island, two or three islands rather than five, and stay within one group so the ferries connect easily. ## The short version

- Choose the **group** first, then the island.
- First time? Naxos, Crete or Paros.
- Short on time? A Saronic island near Athens.
- Hate ferries? Fly straight into Corfu, Crete, Rhodes, Kos, Zakynthos, Kefalonia or Skiathos.
- Want the sweet spot? Go in May or June.
