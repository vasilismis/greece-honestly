The Greek alphabet looks scary, but here is the good news: you already know more of it than you think. Many letters look and sound the same as in English. A handful look familiar but are traps. The rest are new, but there are only ten of them.

Give it ten minutes and you will be sounding out signs at the port, the pharmacy and the airport. Signs in Greece are often written in capital letters, so that is what we will learn here.

:::steps
The easy letters | Eight letters that look and sound like English.
The traps | Six that look familiar but sound different.
New letters and pairs | Ten new letters, then the pairs that make b, d and g.
:::

## Part 1: the easy letters (1 minute)

These look like English letters and sound roughly the same:

<p class="gr">Α Ε Ζ Ι Κ Μ Ο Τ</p>

- **Α** = a, **Ε** = e, **Ζ** = z, **Ι** = i, **Κ** = k, **Μ** = m, **Ο** = o, **Τ** = t

Nothing to learn here. Move on.

## Part 2: the traps (3 minutes)

These six look like English letters but **sound different**. This is where most people trip up, so take your time.

| Greek letter | Looks like | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Η** | H | **i**, as in "machine" |
| **Ρ** | P | **r** |
| **Χ** | X | **ch**, as in Scottish "loch" |
| **Ν** | N | **n**, same as English (but the lower-case form ν looks like a "v") |
| **Υ** | Y | **i**, as in "machine" |
| **Β** | B | **v** |

A good way to remember them: **Ρ is never p**, **Η is never h**, and **Β is never b**. Say that three times.

## Part 3: the new letters (4 minutes)

These ten letters do not exist in English. Each one has a single, consistent sound.

| Letter | Sound | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Γ** | a soft "gh", or "y" before e and i sounds | Like an upside-down L |
| **Δ** | **th** as in "this" | A triangle, "delta" |
| **Θ** | **th** as in "think" | An O with a line through it |
| **Λ** | **l** | An upside-down V |
| **Ξ** | **x** (ks) | Three lines |
| **Π** | **p** | A doorway, "pi" |
| **Σ** | **s** | A sideways M, "sigma" |
| **Φ** | **f** | An O with a stick, "phi" |
| **Ψ** | **ps** | A trident |
| **Ω** | **o** | "Omega", the last letter |

Notice that Greek has two letters for the "th" sounds, which English spells the same way: **Δ** for the soft "this" sound and **Θ** for the harder "think" sound.

## Part 4: the letter pairs (2 minutes)

Some sounds are written with two letters together. Learn these and you can read almost anything.

| Pair | Sound |
|---|---|
| **ΟΥ** | **ou**, as in "you" |
| **ΑΙ** | **e**, as in "pet" |
| **ΕΙ** | **i** |
| **ΟΙ** | **i** |
| **ΜΠ** | **b** |
| **ΝΤ** | **d** |
| **ΓΚ** | **g**, as in "go" |

The last three are the clever bit. Greek has no single letter for "b", "d" or a hard "g", so it uses pairs. Inside a word, ΜΠ and ΝΤ can sound more like "mb" and "nd".

## Part 5: practise on real signs

Now try these. Cover the right-hand column and sound each word out letter by letter.

| Sign | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| <span class="gr">ΚΕΝΤΡΟ</span> | kéndro | centre |
| <span class="gr">ΛΙΜΑΝΙ</span> | limáni | port |
| <span class="gr">ΦΑΡΜΑΚΕΙΟ</span> | farmakío | pharmacy |
| <span class="gr">ΕΞΟΔΟΣ</span> | éxodos | exit |
| <span class="gr">ΑΕΡΟΔΡΟΜΙΟ</span> | aerodrómio | airport |

Let's walk through two of them.

**ΦΑΡΜΑΚΕΙΟ:** Φ is f, Α is a, Ρ is r (not p!), Μ is m, Α is a, Κ is k, then the pair ΕΙ is i, and Ο is o. Put it together: *far-ma-KI-o*. Farmakío. Pharmacy.

**ΕΞΟΔΟΣ:** Ε is e, Ξ is x, Ο is o, Δ is the "th" in "this", Ο is o, Σ is s. *E-xo-dhos*. Éxodos, the exit. You can probably see the English word "exodus" in it.

**ΚΕΝΤΡΟ** shows the ΝΤ pair in action: kéndro, the centre. Follow these signs when you arrive in a new town.

## Lower-case letters

On menus, maps and longer texts you will see lower-case letters too. Many look like their capitals, but a few are different. Two to spot early: **σ** is s, and at the end of a word it is written **ς**. And as mentioned above, **ν** is n, even though it looks like a "v".

Do not worry about learning lower case today. Once you can read capitals, lower case comes quickly.

## Why it is worth ten minutes

Being able to sound out a word gets you a long way:

- You can find the **port**, the **pharmacy** and the **exit** on your own.
- You can match bus and ferry destinations to the names on your ticket.
- Many words on menus become recognisable once you sound them out.
- You will start noticing English words that come from Greek, which is genuinely fun.

Once you can read the letters, the next step is a few words to say out loud. Our list of [20 Greek phrases for your first week](/articles/greek-phrases-for-your-first-week/) is a good place to start.
