Coffee in Greece is not a quick shot at the counter. It is a reason to sit down, often for an hour, often outside. And the menu can be confusing at first, because the most popular drinks are iced, the sugar goes in before you get the cup, and the words are Greek.

Here is what to order, what each drink is, and how to ask for it.

:::facts
sun | Most popular in summer | Iced: freddo espresso and freddo cappuccino
clock | The classic | Frappé, shaken instant coffee

:::

## The four drinks you will see everywhere

| Drink | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Freddo espresso** | Espresso shaken with ice until cold and slightly frothy, served over ice |
| **Freddo cappuccino** | A freddo espresso topped with cold, frothed milk (*afrógala*) |
| **Frappé** | Instant coffee shaken with water and ice into a thick foam, often with milk |
| **Greek coffee** | Finely ground coffee boiled in a small pot and served hot in a small cup, grounds and all |

### Freddo espresso and freddo cappuccino

These are the drinks most Greeks order today, especially in the warm months. The freddo espresso was first made in Athens in 1991. It is espresso shaken with ice, so it arrives cold and lightly foamy. A freddo cappuccino adds a thick layer of cold frothed milk on top.

If you like an iced latte at home, the freddo cappuccino is the one to try first.

### Frappé

The frappé is the older iced classic. It dates back to the 1957 Thessaloniki International Fair, where, according to the most common story, a coffee company employee could not find hot water and shook instant coffee with cold water and ice instead. Today you can ask for it with or without milk.

### Greek coffee

Greek coffee (*ellinikós kafés*) is made by boiling finely ground coffee with water, and sugar if you want it, in a small pot. It is served in a small cup with the grounds settled at the bottom. Sip it slowly and stop before you reach the grounds.

## Sweetness: say it when you order

This is the part that catches visitors out. In Greece the sugar usually goes in while the coffee is being made: into the pot for Greek coffee, and before shaking for a freddo or frappé. So you need to say how sweet you want it **when you order**, not afterwards.

| Say | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Skétos** | No sugar |
| **Métrios** | Medium sweet |
| **Glykós** | Sweet |

The exact amount of sugar varies from café to café, so if you are not sure, start with *métrios* and adjust next time.

:::note Good to know
If you say nothing, you may be asked "*Pos ton thélete?*" (how do you want it?). Just answer *skétos*, *métrios* or *glykós*.
:::

## How to order, word by word

Put the drink, the sweetness and *parakaló* (please) together:

- **Éna freddo espresso métrio, parakaló.** A medium-sweet freddo espresso, please.
- **Éna freddo cappuccino skéto, parakaló.** A freddo cappuccino with no sugar, please.
- **Éna frappé glykó me gála, parakaló.** A sweet frappé with milk, please.
- **Éna elliniko métrio, parakaló.** A medium-sweet Greek coffee, please.

Notice the endings change slightly (*métrios* becomes *métrio*). Do not worry about getting this right; you will be understood.

A few more useful words:

- **me gála**: with milk
- **chorís záchari**: without sugar (another way to say *skétos*)
- **Póso kánei?** How much is it?
- **To logariasmó, parakaló.** The bill, please.
- **Efcharistó.** Thank you.

## Café habits worth knowing

- **Take your time.** Many people sit over one iced coffee for a long while. Nobody will hurry you.
- **Take-away is normal too.** Freddos and frappés are often ordered to go, in a plastic cup with a lid and a straw.
- **Ask for the bill when you are ready.** Just as in a taverna, you usually ask for it.

## Which one should you try first?

- **On a hot afternoon:** a freddo cappuccino, *métrios*.
- **If you want something strong and simple:** a freddo espresso, *skétos*.
- **For the classic Greek café experience:** a frappé, then a Greek coffee another day.
