Island hopping sounds simple: buy a ferry ticket, hop to the next island, repeat. In practice, the most common mistake is trying to see too much. Five islands in ten days means a lot of time on ferries and in ports, and not much time on beaches.

Below are three routes that work, followed by the rules of thumb behind them. Use them as they are, or as a starting point for your own plan.

## First, the rules of thumb

These four rules will save most trips:

1. **At least three nights per island.** Arrival and departure days eat into your time. Two nights often means one real day.
2. **Two or three islands beat five.** You will see more by moving less.
3. **Stay on one ferry line.** Islands in the same group connect easily. Crossing between groups usually means long detours.
4. **Keep the last night near your departure airport.** Ferries can be delayed or cancelled by wind. Do not plan to catch an international flight on the same day as a ferry if you can avoid it.

And one warning that applies to every route here: **ferry times change every year**. Always check the current schedules for your dates before you book anything that cannot be changed.

## Route 1: Classic Cyclades (10–12 days)

The famous islands, in an order that flows.

| Stop | Nights |
|---|---|
| Paros | 3 |
| Naxos | 4 |
| Santorini | 3 |
| Fly back to Athens | – |

**Why it works:** Paros is a relaxed start, with lively harbour towns and good beaches. Naxos is the longest stay for good reason: it is big enough to explore for four nights, with villages, beaches and hills. Santorini comes last, so you finish with the famous views and fly out from there instead of taking another ferry.

**Good for:** first-timers, couples, families.

**Tip:** if your dates are flexible, Santorini is at its best in May or October, when it is less crowded than midsummer. With 10–12 days, you can add a spare night in Athens at either end.

## Route 2: Quiet Cyclades (10 days)

The same island group, a completely different mood. Smaller islands, fewer crowds.

| Stop | Nights |
|---|---|
| Sifnos | 4 |
| Milos | 3 |
| Folegandros | 3 |

**Why it works:** these three islands are quieter than the big names but still have the Cycladic look: white villages, bare hills and clear water. Ten nights split between three islands gives you time to slow down properly.

**Good for:** travellers who want peace and quiet, walkers, second-time visitors.

**Check this first:** connections between small islands do not run every day. **Check that the ferries between Sifnos, Milos and Folegandros run on your dates** before you book accommodation. If a connection does not work, swap the order of the islands rather than adding an extra island.

## Route 3: Ionian by car (10 days)

A green, softer alternative to the Cyclades, mixing islands with a drive on the mainland.

| Stop | Nights | How you get there |
|---|---|---|
| Corfu | 3 | Fly in |
| Lefkada | 4 | Ferry to the mainland, then drive; Lefkada is reached over a floating bridge |
| Kefalonia | 3 | Ferry from Lefkada |
| Home | – | Fly out |

**Why it works:** Corfu and Kefalonia both have airports with flights, so you fly in at one end and out at the other, with no backtracking. Having a car means you can explore beaches and villages at your own pace, and Lefkada's floating bridge means no ferry at all for that leg.

**Good for:** people who like driving, green landscapes and softer winds, and families who want the freedom of a car.

**Check this first:** **make sure the Lefkada–Kefalonia ferry runs on your date.** If you hire a car, confirm with the rental company that you are allowed to take it on ferries.

## When to go

The month changes everything on the islands:

- **May–June:** the sweet spot. Warm, not yet crowded, and all three routes work well.
- **July:** hot and busy, and the meltemi winds blow in the Cyclades. Fine for routes 1 and 2, but expect some lively crossings.
- **August:** peak season. Around 15 August, a Greek national holiday, is the busiest time of all. Book early.
- **September:** warm sea, calmer. A great month for any of these routes.
- **October:** quieter, but some places close on small islands. Route 2 is the one to check carefully.
- **November–March:** small islands go to sleep. Island hopping is hard work in winter; stick to big islands.

## Making your own route

If none of these fits, build your own with the same logic:

1. Pick **one island group**. Our article on [the Greek island groups](/articles/which-greek-island-is-right-for-you/) can help you choose.
2. Choose **two or three islands** in it.
3. Give each **at least three nights**.
4. Make sure your last island is close to your **departure airport**, or has a flight back to Athens.
5. **Check current ferry schedules** for your exact dates.

## The short version

- Fewer islands, longer stays.
- Stay within one island group.
- Leave a buffer before your flight home.
- Always check current ferry times: they change every year.
