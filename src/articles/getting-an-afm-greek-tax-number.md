If you are moving to Greece, renting a flat or opening a bank account, someone will ask for your **AFM** within days. It is your Greek tax identification number, and it opens almost every other door. The good news: you can apply for it online.

This article explains what the AFM is, why it comes first, and how to get it. It is general information, not tax advice.

## What the AFM is, and what it is not

**AFM** stands for *Arithmós Forologikoú Mitróou*, the tax registration number. In English documents it is often called the **TIN** (tax identification number). It is issued by **AADE**, the Greek tax authority.

What the AFM is **not**:

- **It is not a residence permit.** It does not give you the right to live in Greece.
- **It does not make you a Greek tax resident.** Tax residence depends on where your permanent home and centre of life are, and on whether you spend more than 183 days in Greece in any 12-month period.

## Why you need it first

The AFM is the key that unlocks the rest:

- **Renting a flat:** the landlord declares the lease to AADE, and the declaration includes your AFM. You then accept it online.
- **Opening a bank account:** banks ask for it.
- **Many official steps** ask for it too.

That is why our [first 90 days in Greece](/articles/first-90-days-in-greece/) plan puts the AFM at the very start.

## How to apply

:::steps
Apply online | Submit your request through myAADE, AADE's online portal.
Prove who you are | By video call with an AADE officer, or in person at a tax office.
Get your key | Ask for the authentication key so you can use AADE's online services.
:::

### Step 1: apply through myAADE

You submit the request electronically through **myAADE**, AADE's online portal. Non-residents can apply this way too.

### Step 2: confirm your identity

After applying, you confirm who you are in one of two ways:

- a **video call** with an AADE officer (the service is called myAADElive), or
- **in person** at a tax office.

For a foreign non-resident, AADE lists an identity document such as a **passport** among the documents. Have it ready, along with anything else the application asks for.

### Step 3: the authentication key

The AFM on its own is not enough to use AADE's online services. You also need an **authentication key** (*kleidárithmos*), a personal access code. AADE allows anyone who has an AFM, Greek or foreign, resident or not, to apply for one. Ask for it at the same time as your AFM: you will need it to accept your lease online and for most dealings with the tax authority.

## What it costs

The AFM itself is free. If a lawyer or accountant applies for you, they charge for their service, and a notarised authorisation from abroad has its own costs.

## Can someone apply for you?

Yes. Applications can be made by you, by a legal or tax representative, or by an authorised third party. Many people moving from abroad ask a Greek lawyer or accountant to handle it. If you use a representative, ask them exactly what authorisation they need from you, and how it must be signed and certified.

## Which tax office?

The competent tax office is generally based on your residential address. If you do not yet live in Greece, ask AADE or your representative which office applies to you.

## Common questions

**Do I need to live in Greece to get an AFM?**
No. AADE has a procedure for non-residents too.

**Once I have an AFM, do I have to file Greek tax returns?**
It depends on your situation. Having an AFM does not make you a Greek tax resident on its own, but your income, property or residence may create obligations. Ask an accountant.

:::note Good to know
Keep your AFM somewhere safe and easy to find. You will be asked for it again and again: by landlords, banks, utilities and public services.
:::

## Next steps

Once you have your AFM and key, the usual order is: a Greek phone number, a home with a registered lease, a bank account, then your AMKA and residence registration. Read [AMKA explained](/articles/amka-explained/) and [opening a Greek bank account](/articles/opening-a-greek-bank-account/) next.
