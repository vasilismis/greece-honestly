Athens is not one city but dozens of neighbourhoods, each with its own pace. Where you rent shapes your daily life: whether you walk to the bakery or drive to the supermarket, whether your evenings are lively or quiet, whether you see the Acropolis or the sea.

This guide compares five areas that newcomers often look at. It is a starting point, not a ranking. Rents and availability change quickly, so we do not quote prices here: check current listings for the area you like.

## At a glance

| Area | What it is like | Often chosen by |
|---|---|---|
| **Koukaki** | Central, walkable, close to the Acropolis | People who want to walk everywhere |
| **Pangrati** | Quieter, leafy, good local tavernas | Families and long-term residents |
| **Kypseli** | Central, lived-in, more affordable than many central areas | Renters on a budget who want to stay central |
| **Glyfada** | Seaside suburb, upscale | Families, people who want the coast |
| **Kifisia** | Leafy northern suburb, family-oriented | Families wanting space and greenery |

## Koukaki

Koukaki sits just south of the Acropolis. Its big draw is that you can walk to the historic centre, and it has plenty of cafés and small shops. It has become very popular with newcomers and visitors, and guides note that it has become one of the pricier central areas in recent years.

**Suits you if:** you want to live centrally and walk most places.
**Think about:** the pull of short-term lets and visitors in a central area; ask about noise and how many flats in the building are let to tourists.

## Pangrati

Pangrati is close to the centre but feels calmer. Guides describe it as leafy and residential, with good local tavernas, and often recommend it for families and people staying long term.

**Suits you if:** you want a neighbourhood feel without leaving the centre.
**Think about:** visit at different times of day to get a feel for each street.

## Kypseli

Kypseli, north of the centre, is usually described as one of the more affordable options close to the middle of the city. It is a lived-in, everyday neighbourhood rather than a tourist one.

**Suits you if:** you want to be central on a tighter budget.
**Think about:** quality varies a lot from street to street and building to building, so view carefully.

## Glyfada

Glyfada is on the coast south of the centre, and it is one of the more upscale areas. It is popular with families, including those choosing international schools in the area.

**Suits you if:** you want the sea nearby and a suburban pace.
**Think about:** rents are among the higher ones in the city, and the trip into the centre takes longer.

## Kifisia

Kifisia, in the north, is a leafy, family-oriented suburb. It is often described as one of the more expensive areas for larger flats.

**Suits you if:** you want greenery and space, and you do not need to be in the centre every day.
**Think about:** check your commute to work or school before you commit.

:::quote
Visit the street at night and on a weekday morning before you sign. A neighbourhood can feel very different at 8am and 11pm.
:::

## How to choose

:::steps
Map your week | Where will you work, shop, study or meet people most often?
Visit twice | Walk the area on a weekday morning and on an evening.
Check the flat | Heating, damp, noise and the building, before you talk about rent.
:::

Questions to ask yourself:

- **Do you need a car?** Central areas are easier without one; suburbs often are not.
- **Children?** Check the distance to schools before anything else.
- **Working from home?** Look for light, a quiet room and a reliable internet connection.
- **Summer heat:** ask how the flat stays cool, and whether air conditioning is included.

## Before you sign

Whichever area you choose, the paperwork is the same. You will need an **AFM**, the lease must be **declared to AADE** by the landlord and accepted by you online, and the minimum term for a main residence is **three years**.
