Greece offers retirees a special tax regime: a flat 7% on foreign income instead of the normal rates, which go up to 44%. It is one of the reasons many pensioners look at Greece in the first place.

The rules were updated in 2026, and the deadlines matter more than ever. This article explains who qualifies, when to apply and the traps to avoid. It is general information, not tax advice.

:::facts
percent | Flat rate | 7% on all foreign income
calendar | For up to | 15 tax years
doc | Apply by | 31 October
euro | Pay by | Last working day of December, in one go
:::

## The rules in brief

The regime is set out in article 5B of the Greek Income Tax Code. It was updated by Law 5313/2026 (article 94) and by AADE decision A.1192/2026, published in the Government Gazette (B' 5767) on 24 September 2026. AADE is the Greek tax authority.

- **Rate:** a flat 7% on **all** your foreign-source income, not only the pension.
- **Length:** up to 15 tax years. It cannot be extended.
- **Foreign tax:** tax you pay abroad can be credited, but any excess is not refunded.

## Who qualifies

You need to tick all four boxes:

1. You receive a foreign pension.
2. You move your tax residence to Greece.
3. You were not a Greek tax resident in five of the previous six years.
4. You are moving from a country that has a tax administrative cooperation agreement with Greece.

As a reminder, you are generally a Greek tax resident if Greece is your permanent home or centre of life, or if you spend more than 183 days there in any 12-month period.

You cannot combine the 7% regime with the article 5A regime, the €100,000 flat tax for investors.

## How much could you save?

Take a €40,000 foreign pension.

| | Tax |
|---|---|
| Normal 2026 scale (9% + 20% + 26% + 34% on each €10,000) | about €8,900 before credits |
| 7% regime | €2,800 |

That is a big gap, which is exactly why the deadlines below are worth taking seriously.

## The 31 October deadline and the 2 July rule

Before Law 5313/2026, the application deadline was **31 March**. AADE decision A.1192/2026 moved it to **31 October**. So under the current rules, the application deadline is **31 October** of the year you want the regime to start. Which years you can choose depends on when you moved.

| You moved to Greece… | You can join… | Apply by… |
|---|---|---|
| on or before 2 July | for the year you arrived, **or** from the following year | 31 October of the year you choose to start |
| after 2 July | from the following year | 31 October of that following year |

A few more dates to know:

- **30 November:** if documents are missing from your application, you can send them by this date.
- **Within 60 days:** AADE decides on your application, at the latest by the last working day of December.

If you have just missed 31 October, do not panic. Depending on when you moved, the next year may still be open to you. Look at the table above, then check your own case with an accountant.

## The payment catch: one instalment, no plans

:::note The trap that hurts most
- You pay the 7% tax in **one instalment** by the last working day of December.
- There are **no instalment plans**.
- If you do not pay in full, the regime ends from that year.

So keep the money aside before December comes round.
:::

## How to apply

You have three options:

- **Online** through myAADE, under "My requests".
- **By post or courier.**
- **In person**, by appointment.

The competent office depends on where you live:

- **KEFODE Attica:** Attica, plus the islands of Kea, Kythnos, Andros and Milos.
- **KEFODE Thessaloniki:** the rest of Greece.

Ask your accountant which documents to prepare for your situation. Proof of your pension and of your previous tax residence are the obvious starting points.

## Leaving the regime

You can withdraw by 31 October of any year. But there is no way back: once you leave, you can **never** re-enter the regime. Think carefully before you withdraw, for example if your income changes.

## Watch out for tax treaties

Tax treaties between Greece and other countries can change who taxes some pensions. Government-service pensions, for example, are often taxed only by the country that pays them. That can affect how much of your income actually falls under the 7%.

Also check the residence rules of the country you are leaving. Moving your tax residence to Greece does not automatically end your tax ties elsewhere.

## Quick checklist

- Confirm you meet all four conditions.
- Work out when you will (or did) move, relative to 2 July.
- Note the deadline: 31 October of the year you want to start.
- Keep any missing documents ready for 30 November at the latest.
- Set aside the full 7% for payment by the last working day of December.
- Check treaty rules for each of your pensions.
- Get advice from a Greek accountant for your own figures.
