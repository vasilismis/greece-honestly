You can travel in Greece without speaking a word of Greek. Plenty of people speak English, especially in tourist areas. But a few words of Greek change the mood of almost every conversation. People smile, relax and often reply with a few words of English to meet you halfway.

Here are twenty phrases that will cover most of your first week. You do not need to learn them all at once. Start with the first five.

## How to read the pronunciation

- The **accent mark** (é, á, ó, í) shows which syllable to stress. Stress matters in Greek: say it louder and a little longer. *Efcharistó* is "ef-ha-ree-STO", not "EF-ha-ree-sto".
- **"ch"** is the throaty sound in the Scottish word "loch", never the "ch" in "chair".
- Vowels are short and clear: **a** as in "cat", **e** as in "pet", **i** as in "machine", **o** as in "hot", **u** as in "put".
- Don't worry about getting it perfect. A warm attempt is always welcome.

## The essentials

| Greek | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kaliméra** | Good morning |
| **Kalispéra** | Good evening |
| **Yiá sas** | Hello (polite) |
| **Efcharistó** | Thank you |
| **Parakaló** | Please / you're welcome |
| **Ne** | Yes |
| **Óchi** | No |
| **Signómi** | Sorry |

**Kaliméra** is for the morning; switch to **kalispéra** later in the day.

**Yiá sas** is the polite hello, good for shopkeepers, waiters and anyone older than you. It also works as goodbye.

**Parakaló** is the most useful word in the language. It means "please", it means "you're welcome" when someone thanks you, and you will hear it in shops when someone is ready to serve you.

:::note Good to know
Watch out for **ne**. It means **yes**, even though it sounds a bit like "no". And **óchi**, with the throaty "ch", means no. Mixing these two up is the classic beginner's mistake.
:::

## Shopping and getting around

| Greek | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Póso kánei?** | How much is it? |
| **Pu íne…?** | Where is…? |
| **Den katalavéno** | I don't understand |
| **Miláte angliká?** | Do you speak English? |

**Póso kánei?** works in markets, kiosks and shops. Point at the thing you want and ask.

**Pu íne…?** is followed by whatever you are looking for. Point at a map or say the place name: *"Pu íne to limáni?"* (Where is the port?)

**Den katalavéno** and **Miláte angliká?** are your safety net. Most people will be happy to switch to English, and you will have made a good start by asking in Greek.

## At the taverna

| Greek | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Éna neró, parakaló** | A water, please |
| **To logariasmó, parakaló** | The bill, please |
| **Kalí órexi** | Enjoy your meal |
| **Yiá mas** | Cheers |

**Kalí órexi** is what you will hear when your food arrives. You can say it back to the people at your table.

**Yiá mas** means "to our health". Raise your glass and say it before the first sip.

For more on eating out, see [what to order in a Greek taverna](/articles/what-to-order-in-a-greek-taverna/).

## Emergencies

| Greek | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Voíthia!** | Help! |

Learn this one even if you learn nothing else. And save the emergency number **112** in your phone: it is the European emergency number and works in Greece.

## Phrases with a bit of Greek character

| Greek | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kaló taxídi** | Have a good trip |
| **Sigá-sigá** | Slowly, slowly |
| **Kaló mína** | Happy new month |

**Kaló taxídi** is what people say when you are setting off. You will hear it when you check out of your hotel.

**Sigá-sigá** is almost a philosophy. It means "slowly, slowly", "take it easy", "no rush". You will hear it a lot, and by the end of your trip you might find yourself saying it too.

**Kaló mína** is a lovely Greek habit. On the **1st of every month**, people wish each other a happy new month. Say it to the person at the bakery on the 1st and watch their face light up.

## How to practise

- **Learn five a day.** Start with the essentials, then add the others.
- **Use them straight away.** Say *kaliméra* at breakfast and *efcharistó* every time someone helps you.
- **Listen.** You will hear these phrases all around you. Copy the rhythm and the stress.
- **Learn the alphabet.** Even a basic idea of Greek letters helps you read signs and menus. Try our article on [how to read Greek signs](/articles/how-to-read-greek-signs-alphabet/).
