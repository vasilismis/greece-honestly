Ferries are how most people get around the Greek islands. The system works, but it has its own habits: schedules that change every year, tickets that may be electronic or paper, and wind that can upset the best-laid plan.

This guide covers how to book and what to know before you sail.

:::

## Where to book

You have three options:

1. **The ferry company's own website.** Each operator sells tickets for its own boats.
2. **A booking site or app.** Aggregators such as Ferryhopper show several companies' sailings in one search, which helps when you are comparing times or building a multi-island route.
3. **At the port or a local travel agent.** Ticket booths at the port are typically open at least an hour before departure. In busy periods, do not rely on buying at the last minute.

Whichever you use, compare the **departure and arrival times**, the **type of boat** (fast or conventional) and the **port**: big cities can have more than one, so check which one your boat leaves from.

## E-tickets or paper?

Almost all ferry companies in Greece now provide **e-tickets**. A few cases still need a printout or a paper ticket collected at the port. Read your booking confirmation carefully: it will tell you whether you can board with the e-ticket on your phone or need to collect a paper ticket first.

## What to bring

- **Your passport or ID card**, the original. Tickets are in your name and may be checked.
- **Proof of any discount** you booked, such as a student card. Without it you may be asked to pay the difference.
- Your ticket or booking reference.

## When to be at the port

Standard advice is to arrive **about an hour before departure**. Some companies' terms ask foot passengers to be **on board** at least half an hour before the scheduled departure, and vehicles earlier. If you have a car, or you need to collect a paper ticket, allow more time.

Officers check tickets as you board and sometimes during the voyage, so keep yours handy.

:::steps
Book | On the operator's site, an app, or at the port.
Check | E-ticket or paper? Which port? Your name matches your ID?
Arrive | About an hour early, with your passport or ID.
:::

## When the wind blows

Ferries can be **delayed or cancelled by wind**, especially in the Cyclades in July and August, when the meltemi wind blows. That is why our island-hopping rules say:

- **Keep the last night near your departure airport.** Do not plan to catch an international flight on the same day as a ferry if you can avoid it.
- **Stay on one ferry line.** Islands in the same group connect easily; crossing between groups usually means long detours.

If your sailing is cancelled, contact the company or the agent you booked with, and keep an eye on your email and phone for messages.

## Out of season

Ferries run all year, but outside summer there are **fewer sailings**, and some routes and connections stop after the middle of October. Small islands may have only a few boats a week. In autumn and winter, check schedules especially carefully and keep your plans flexible. See [Greek islands in October and November](/articles/greek-islands-in-october-and-november/).

:::note Good to know
Around 15 August, a Greek national holiday, is peak season for ferries as well as islands. Book early for that week.
:::

## Planning a multi-island trip

- At least **three nights per island**.
- **Two or three islands** beat five.
- Stay on **one ferry line**.
- Keep the **last night near your departure airport**.
